Audit PrivacyLens

A privacy extension deserves scrutiny too. PrivacyLens has powerful permissions. This page explains their real use; it is not a self-awarded safety score.

To compare them with Chrome, open chrome://extensions, find PrivacyLens and choose Details. The extension cannot revoke or narrow Chrome’s bundled powers itself.

Local checks do not send data outside the browser. VirusTotal is optional and separately confirmed. No browsing history, scan result or extension inventory is saved.

Why each permission exists

Chrome sometimes grants more capability than this implementation uses. Expand a permission to see the difference.

Where data goes

“Persisted” means saved beyond a browser restart or extension reload. Temporary memory may outlive a popup: one current download/navigation snapshot lasts at most five minutes, often less when the worker stops. Session key/quota memory lasts until browser restart or extension reload/disable/update.

The optional API key is authentication: it leaves the browser during a confirmed lookup. Remember saves only the key in your local profile, without sync or PrivacyLens encryption. Forget removes the extension’s copies, not the key at VirusTotal.

VirusTotal says queried indicators may be scanned and included in its shared dataset. Do not check confidential hostnames. A failed or cancelled network request may already have shared the hostname/key. No results are cached; no full URLs, typed values, cookies, inventory or files are sent.

Session quota counters contain only an anonymous day, count and two deadlines; no per-request log, hostname or report. Public API limits and account restrictions still apply.
