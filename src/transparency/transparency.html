Audit PrivacyLens

You should be able to question a privacy extension too. PrivacyLens asks for powerful permissions. Here is how it uses them, rather than giving itself a trust score.

To compare them with Chrome, open chrome://extensions, find PrivacyLens and choose Details. The extension cannot revoke or narrow Chrome’s bundled powers itself.

Local checks do not send data outside the browser. VirusTotal is optional and separately confirmed. No browsing history, scan result or extension inventory is saved.

Why each permission exists

Chrome sometimes grants more access than PrivacyLens uses. Open a permission below to see the difference.

Where data goes

“Persisted” means kept after a browser restart or extension reload. Some temporary data lasts longer than the popup. One current download or navigation snapshot stays in memory for up to five minutes, often less if the background worker stops. The session key and request counters last until the browser restarts or the extension is reloaded, disabled or updated.

Your API key is sent to VirusTotal to authenticate a confirmed lookup. Remember saves only the key in your browser profile. PrivacyLens does not sync or encrypt it. Forget removes PrivacyLens’s copies; it does not revoke the key at VirusTotal.

VirusTotal may scan a hostname you check and add it to its shared dataset. Avoid confidential hostnames. A failed or cancelled request may already have sent the hostname and key. PrivacyLens keeps no reports and sends no full URLs, typed values, cookies, extension list or files.

Session quota counters contain only an anonymous day, count and two deadlines; no per-request log, hostname or report. Public API limits and account restrictions still apply.
